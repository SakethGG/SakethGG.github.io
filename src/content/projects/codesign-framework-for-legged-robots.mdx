Track 1: 

CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) is a derivative-free stochastic optimizer
for continuous, non-convex, and potentially noisy objective functions — well suited to expensive
black-box problems where gradients are unavailable, unreliable, or hard to compute. It works by
maintaining a multivariate Gaussian search distribution and adapting it generation over generation:

- **Sample** — candidate solutions are drawn from a Gaussian parameterized by a mean vector, a
  global step size, and a covariance matrix.
- **Evaluate & select** — each candidate is scored against the objective function, and the
  highest-performing ones are used to update the distribution mean.
- **Adapt the covariance** — the covariance matrix learns the scaling and correlations between
  optimization variables, effectively capturing the local geometry of the search landscape.
- **Adapt the step size** — a separate mechanism controls the overall exploration radius, balancing
  global exploration against local refinement.

Repeated over generations, the search distribution evolves toward regions of increasingly
favorable fitness.


Track 2: 


A 2-DOF planar leg testbed for co-designing robot link lengths `[L1, L2]` against a torque/tracking
cost, following De Vincenti, Kang & Coors (ICRA 2023). It couples two nested optimization loops:

- **Inner loop** — a per-timestep QP (solved via OSQP) computes joint accelerations and torques
  jointly for an operational-space PD task-tracking controller, simulated forward in MuJoCo.
- **Outer loop** — LBFGS++ optimizes the link lengths against the accumulated cost (squared mean
  actuator effort), using gradients from analytic KKT differentiation of the QP — an OptNet-style
  implicit differentiation of the inner controller — plus finite-difference-verified dynamics
  partials.

The interesting part is the KKT sensitivity: instead of treating the inner-loop controller as a
black box and finite-differencing through the whole simulation, the gradient of the outer cost
with respect to the design variables is propagated analytically through the QP's optimality
conditions.
