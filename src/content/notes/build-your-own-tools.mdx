Software as a product is no longer a viable company worth the trouble. Now almost anyone can build their own tools for their personalised tasks and use cases. You don't need to pay other parties to use their software; you can just build it from scratch and add relevant features for yourself. The best part is that everything can be done locally on your hardware, so you don't need to share your personal info like mail and phone number, which the parties will sell, and hence you get a lot of spam calls and junk mails.

I encourage everyone to build their own stuff. There is a GitHub repo which is getting popular now called "Reverse Engineer anything". You can practically build your own replica of anything and everything. So crazy and nuts. But who is going to maintain the free replicas of the paid software? If we have a bunch of people using the tool in our organisation, then just put one guy who maintains it, or use agents for that too!! If a person has a problem, he just posts it in some window and the agent will iteratively solve the problem.

But it is actually not legal to replicate a software, as many have a clause in the terms and services when you sign up. The user agreement explicitly prohibits reverse engineering, monitoring inputs/outputs, or analyzing their systems to recreate them.

If you (or your AI agents) logged into a software, analyzed its UI, tracked its tools, or fed its documentation into an LLM to clone it, you breached a binding contract. So be careful. Or you can just build your own tool with your own knowledge.

I developed this tool for myself. As a researcher, I can't keep up with the new research papers which come out every single day. This acceleration in research is definitely because of AI making it extremely easy to take your idea from concept to an actual working model. So my tool sweeps the internet for papers in my domain and research interests, along with research from profs whom you follow, and summarises it for me using Claude/GPT. I can also plan my projects and add my future research direction ideas. It also keeps track of all the conference deadlines so I don't have to.
